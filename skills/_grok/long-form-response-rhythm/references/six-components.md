# Six Musical Components

These are design lenses, not a fixed template and not vocabulary that must appear in the Human-facing answer.

## Beat

Each section or meaning unit carries one semantic beat. When the section ends, understanding has advanced.

Failure — headings change while content repeats; one section packs unrelated jobs.

## Phrase

Humans read meaning-chunks, not isolated sentences. A working phrase is claim → reason → Reality example → a deeper point → a small close. Close the phrase before starting the next.

Guard — keep paragraph unity. Do not perform tempo with a run of tiny sentences. Mix long and short according to meaning.

## Rest

Rest is landing space after density, not missing content.

Place a short semantic beat after a heavy analysis so the next block can be received. Do not insert a mechanical one-liner after every paragraph. Do not confuse blank lines with tempo.

## Dynamics

Do not write the whole answer at one intensity.

- Confirmed fact — clear
- Interpretation — slightly softer
- Hypothesis — labeled as hypothesis
- Core discovery — strong
- Background detail — quiet
- Reality step — brief landing

If everything is bold and "most important," nothing is.

## Motif

A long answer needs a melody the Human already holds, so deep branches can return. Use the current mission keyword if one exists. Do not compete with it by promoting Response Rhythm itself.

## Cadence

A major movement should reach Reality when the theory has drifted away from it.

Meaning → so what → Reality.

Not every section needs a next step. A long theoretical movement must not end in mid-air.
