# Audit Agent Instructions

Improve instructions so capable agents can complete the intended work with appropriate discretion. Optimize decision quality and verified completion, not brevity, token consumption, or freedom alone. Do not turn ordinary work into an unsolicited instruction audit.

## Bind the audit

Read the actual instructions in scope and relevant observed failures. Distinguish source text, user feedback, and your inferred mechanism. If the target is unclear, use visible context to identify the smallest useful scope; ask only when the choice materially changes the assignment.

Treat audits and plan-only requests as analysis. For authorized revisions, complete the scoped edit and proportionate verification. Use the available skill-creation workflow when creating or installing skills; this audit supplies decision criteria, not installation mechanics or additional permissions.

## Apply relevant lenses

Choose the lenses that change this task; do not perform a fixed checklist ritual.

- **Discovery:** Keep skill descriptions brief and specific to the actual trigger. Remove broad neighboring-topic triggers that cause irrelevant invocation. Check overlap before adding another skill.
- **Context:** Keep essential shared guidance in the entrypoint. Move substantial conditional material to clearly routed references when that reduces irrelevant reading. Keep a simple skill self-contained; do not split files merely to demonstrate progressive disclosure.
- **Discretion:** Replace obsolete itineraries with outcomes and decision criteria where methods can vary. Retain exact order, source-reading contracts, schemas, and deterministic steps where they protect a concrete requirement. Relevance-based reading does not waive an explicitly required full read.
- **Verification:** Scale checks to changed behavior and remaining risk. Replace redundant test exhortations with meaningful completion evidence. Stop optional testing once sufficiently verified; do not remove required gates or claim validation that did not occur.
- **Authority:** Separate safe actions already authorized from genuinely new scope, production effects, or irreversible decisions. Remove repeated permission loops within approved scope. A model's capability or alignment reputation never grants additional authority or bypasses access controls, Root, Human correction, or applicable guards.
- **Persistence:** Make the requested outcome include necessary implementation, inspection, fixes, and verification. Remove first-pass review stops only when they are unnecessary for the actual task. Preserve plan-only and explicit STOP boundaries.
- **Exploration:** State what remains worth exploring and what would justify convergence. A finite Human-facing action does not limit internal alternatives or required operations to one. Preserve unresolved benefits; do not convert useful exploration into endless redesign.
- **Portability:** Prefer one model-neutral core. Add model-specific guidance only for relevant evidence, with its scope explicit. Do not encode claims of AGI, infallibility, unlimited resources, or guaranteed resets as operating assumptions.

## Deliver the useful change

Explain the material problem, the affected instruction, its practical consequence, and the proposed or applied correction. Preserve domain meaning, adopted definitions, and necessary keywords. No mandatory score, table, section count, or target length.

For behavior-sensitive changes, use a small realistic check that could expose misrouting, early stopping, or boundary loss. Separate document consistency, observed agent behavior, remote persistence, and real-world outcomes. Return to the user's substantive task after the scoped improvement; do not recursively audit the whole environment.

## Source and refresh

Use [source-notes.md](references/source-notes.md) only when attribution, article rationale, or refresh decisions matter. Routine application needs no web reread. Revisit official sources when the user requests current guidance, supplies a revision, or a material discrepancy changes the recommendation.
